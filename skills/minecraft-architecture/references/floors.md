# Floors and paving

Treat the floor as part of circulation, structure, interior use and light. Measure bounds, Y level, orientation, entrances, columns, stairs, furnishings, support, redstone and protection zones before choosing a motif.

Pick a base, secondary, border and restrained accent. A 70–85% base / 10–25% secondary / up to 5–10% accent split is a starting palette, not a rule. Use larger low-noise panels in public halls, directional bands in corridors/stations, axial/framed motifs at formal entries, and constrained natural variation outdoors. Define module size, origin, rotation, mirror and repeat. Absorb leftover width at borders so the central motif remains intact.

Place seams at room and material transitions. Check every entrance, corner, column base, stair opening and furniture footprint. Make a top-down module or small exact-size sample only when repetition could multiply an error. Record block IDs, properties and quantities. Carpet, slabs, stairs and lamps need a valid support/collision plan. Integrate light positions before laying the full floor; do not perforate a finished surface with random emitters.

Review both plan and player-height views. Confirm clear routes, threshold continuity, readable pattern scale and night legibility. A plain floor still needs a deliberate material, edge and transition. The supplied floor guide's pattern taxonomy is not a verified reproduction of every linked video example.
